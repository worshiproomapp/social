Bible verses about hope
By the Worship Room team · November 10, 2026
In the Bible, hope isn't wishful thinking. It's confident expectation, anchored in who God is and what He has promised. Romans 15:13 calls Him "the God of hope," Lamentations 3:22–23 finds hope in mercies that are new every morning, and Hebrews 6:19 describes hope as "an anchor of the soul."

When hope feels far away
“Why are you in despair, my soul? Why are you disturbed within me? Hope in God! For I shall still praise him, the saving help of my countenance, and my God.”
Psalm 42:11
The psalmist talks to his own soul. Sometimes hope starts with telling yourself the truth out loud.
“This I recall to my mind; therefore I have hope. It is because of Yahweh’s loving kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his mercies don’t fail. They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.”
Lamentations 3:21–23
These words were written in the ruins of Jerusalem. Hope here isn't denial; it's remembering God in the middle of loss.
“I wait for Yahweh. My soul waits. I hope in his word.”
Psalm 130:5
Waiting and hoping are the same posture in Hebrew poetry: leaning forward toward God.
Hope for your future
“For I know the thoughts that I think toward you,’ says Yahweh, ‘thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you hope and a future.”
Jeremiah 29:11
Spoken to exiles who would wait seventy years. It's a promise about God's good intent, even when the timeline is long.
“We know that all things work together for good for those who love God, for those who are called according to his purpose.”
Romans 8:28
Not that everything is good, but that God works in all things for good for those who love Him.
“…but those who wait for Yahweh will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.”
Isaiah 40:31
Waiting on God isn't passive. It's where your strength gets renewed.
Hope that holds in suffering
“Not only this, but we also rejoice in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces perseverance; and perseverance, proven character; and proven character, hope; and hope doesn’t disappoint us, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us.”
Romans 5:3–5
Paul traces a path: suffering, perseverance, character, hope. And this hope doesn't disappoint, because God's love is already in your heart.
“For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which will be revealed toward us.”
Romans 8:18
Paul knew real suffering. He still believed what's coming outweighs it.
“This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, a hope both sure and steadfast…”
Hebrews 6:19
An anchor doesn't stop the storm. It keeps you from drifting in it.
Hope that lasts forever
“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to his great mercy caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead…”
1 Peter 1:3
A "living" hope, because it rests on a risen Savior, not on how things are going.
“He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more; neither will there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain any more. The first things have passed away.”
Revelation 21:4
The final picture of Christian hope: no more tears, no more death, no more pain.
“…let’s hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering; for he who promised is faithful.”
Hebrews 10:23
Your grip may be weak. The One who promised isn't.
“Now may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that you may abound in hope in the power of the Holy Spirit.”
Romans 15:13
Hope here is something God fills you with, not something you have to manufacture.
A short prayer for hope
God of hope, I'm tired of waiting and I don't see the way forward yet. Remind me that Your mercies are new this morning, and that You haven't stopped working. Fill me with joy and peace as I trust You, until hope overflows. Amen.
Ways to let these verses take root
Start your morning with Lamentations 3:22–23. It was written for days that begin heavy.
Keep a list of answered prayers. Looking back at what God has already carried you through is one of the oldest ways to build hope.
Read a psalm of lament. Psalms 42, 13, and 77 show that honest sorrow and hope can live in the same prayer.
Frequently asked questions
What does the Bible say about hope?
The Bible describes hope as confident expectation grounded in God's character and promises, not uncertain wishing. It calls hope "an anchor of the soul" (Hebrews 6:19), says hope "doesn't disappoint us" (Romans 5:5), and points to a future where God will wipe away every tear (Revelation 21:4).
What is the most famous Bible verse about hope?
Jeremiah 29:11 is the best known: "thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you hope and a future." Romans 15:13 and Lamentations 3:22–23 are also among the most shared.
How do I find hope when I feel hopeless?
Scripture models honesty first. Many psalms begin in despair before turning to hope. Tell God exactly how you feel, read a psalm of lament, and lean on others for support. If hopelessness lasts or feels overwhelming, please reach out to someone you trust or a professional.
