How to pray: a simple guide for beginners
How to pray: a simple guide for beginners
By the Worship Room team · November 10, 2026
To pray, simply talk to God honestly, as you would to someone who loves you. There's no special language or posture required. A simple pattern many people use is ACTS: Adoration (praise God for who He is), Confession (admit what you've done wrong), Thanksgiving (thank Him for His gifts), and Supplication (ask for what you and others need). Jesus also gave the Lord's Prayer as a model.

What prayer is
Prayer is conversation with God. It can be spoken, whispered, written, or silent. It can be long or a single sentence. The Bible's prayers include praise, thanks, questions, complaints, confessions, and cries for help. God isn't looking for polished words; He's looking for you.
“Trust in him at all times, you people. Pour out your heart before him. God is a refuge for us. Selah.”
Psalm 62:8
"Pour out your heart" is an invitation to complete honesty.
“…we don’t know how to pray as we ought. But the Spirit himself makes intercession for us…”
Romans 8:26
Even when you don't know what to say, you're not on your own.
A simple pattern: ACTS
Adoration
Start by praising God for who He is: good, faithful, loving, powerful. "God, You are good, and You've been faithful to me."
Confession
Admit where you've fallen short, without hiding or excusing it. "Forgive me for the harsh words I said today."
Thanksgiving
Thank God for specific gifts, big and small. "Thank You for my friend's call, and for a good night's sleep."
Supplication
Ask for what you and others need. "Please give me wisdom for this decision, and heal my mom."
The prayer Jesus taught
When the disciples asked Jesus, "Lord, teach us to pray," He gave them what we now call the Lord's Prayer. It moves from honoring God, to His Kingdom and will, to daily needs, forgiveness, and protection. You can pray it word for word or use each line as a prompt. Read the Lord's Prayer line by line.
Practical tips for getting started
Start small. Two minutes a day is better than an hour once a month.
Pick a time and place. Morning coffee, your commute, or before bed. Jesus often withdrew to quiet places to pray (Luke 5:16).
Keep a list. Write down people and needs you're praying for, and note when prayers are answered.
Pray Scripture. The Psalms are a prayer book. Read one and make its words your own.
Write it down. A prayer journal helps when your mind wanders.
Don't worry about doing it wrong. Jesus warned against empty, showy prayers (Matthew 6:5–8), not simple, honest ones.
Short prayers to get you started
When you don't know what to say
God, I don't really know how to do this, but I want to know You. Help me.
Thanks
Thank You for this day and for every good thing in it.
For help
Lord, I need You. Please help me with this.
For others
Father, be with the people I love today. Keep them safe and show them Your love.

Frequently asked questions
How do I start praying to God?
Just start talking to Him honestly. Tell God what you're thankful for, what's on your mind, and what you need. You can use a simple pattern like ACTS (Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Supplication) or pray the Lord's Prayer from Matthew 6:9–13.
Is there a right way to pray?
There's no single right formula. Jesus warned against praying to impress others or using empty repetition (Matthew 6:5–8), and He gave the Lord's Prayer as a model. Honest, humble, regular prayer is what matters most.
How long should I pray?
There's no required length. Some of the Bible's prayers are a single sentence. Starting with a few minutes a day and growing from there is a good approach.
