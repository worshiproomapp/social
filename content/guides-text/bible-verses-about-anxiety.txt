Bible verses about anxiety
By the Worship Room team · November 10, 2026
The Bible speaks to anxiety directly and gently. Philippians 4:6–7 invites you to bring every worry to God in prayer and promises His peace will guard your heart. Jesus tells His followers not to be anxious about tomorrow (Matthew 6:34), and 1 Peter 5:7 says to cast all your worries on Him, "because he cares for you."

When your mind won't stop racing
“In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.”
Philippians 4:6–7
Paul doesn't say "stop feeling anxious." He gives you something to do with it: turn each worry into a request, and say thank you while you wait.
“In the multitude of my thoughts within me, your comforts delight my soul.”
Psalm 94:19
The psalmist had a "multitude" of thoughts too. God's comfort met him in the middle of them, not after they stopped.
“You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.”
Isaiah 26:3
Peace here isn't the absence of trouble. It's a mind that keeps returning to God.
When you're worried about tomorrow
“Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.”
Matthew 6:34
Jesus isn't dismissing tomorrow's problems. He's giving you permission to leave them in tomorrow.
“Therefore I tell you, don’t be anxious for your life: what you will eat, or what you will drink; nor yet for your body, what you will wear. Isn’t life more than food, and the body more than clothing? See the birds of the sky, that they don’t sow, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you of much more value than they?”
Matthew 6:25–26
Look up at the birds. If God provides for them without a plan or a barn, He has not forgotten you.
“Anxiety in a man’s heart weighs it down, but a kind word makes it glad.”
Proverbs 12:25
An honest proverb: anxiety is heavy. One kind word, from God or from a friend, can lighten it.
When you feel overwhelmed
“…casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.”
1 Peter 5:7
"Casting" is a strong word. You don't hand worries over gently; you throw them, because they're too heavy to hold.
“Cast your burden on Yahweh and he will sustain you. He will never allow the righteous to be moved.”
Psalm 55:22
A burden doesn't always disappear. Here, God promises to sustain you while you carry it.
“Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; and you will find rest for your souls.”
Matthew 11:28–29
Jesus describes Himself as gentle and humble. He isn't adding to your load; He's offering to share it.
When anxiety turns into fear
“Don’t you be afraid, for I am with you. Don’t be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you. Yes, I will help you. Yes, I will uphold you with the right hand of my righteousness.”
Isaiah 41:10
Five promises in one verse: I am with you, I am your God, I will strengthen you, help you, and hold you up.
“When I am afraid, I will put my trust in you.”
Psalm 56:3
Notice it says "when," not "if." Being afraid isn't a failure of faith. Trusting God in it is faith.
“Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you; not as the world gives, I give to you. Don’t let your heart be troubled, neither let it be fearful.”
John 14:27
The peace Jesus gives doesn't depend on circumstances lining up. That's what makes it different from the world's.
“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”
Psalm 46:1
"A very present help" means He's not on the way. He's already here.
A short prayer for anxiety
Father, my thoughts are running ahead of me. I can't fix all of this, and You haven't asked me to. I'm handing You each worry, one at a time. Guard my heart and mind with Your peace, the kind I can't explain, and help me take the next small step with You. Amen.
How to hold on to these verses
Pick one verse for the week. Write it somewhere you'll see it often, and read it slowly when worry rises.
Pray it back to God. Turn Philippians 4:6 into your own words: "God, here's what I'm worried about, and here's what I'm thankful for."
Breathe with it. Breathe in on the first half of a verse and out on the second, like "When I am afraid / I will put my trust in you."
Write it down. Putting a worry on paper next to a verse helps you see it in proportion.
Faith and professional help aren't opposites. If anxiety is shaping most of your days, a doctor or a licensed counselor can help, and many Christians find that care to be one of the ways God answers prayer.
Frequently asked questions
What does the Bible say about anxiety?
The Bible treats anxiety as a normal human struggle, not a sin to hide. It invites you to bring your worries to God in prayer (Philippians 4:6–7), to cast them on Him because He cares for you (1 Peter 5:7), and to live one day at a time (Matthew 6:34), and it promises His peace in return.
What is the best Bible verse for anxiety?
Philippians 4:6–7 is the most widely used, because it gives you something to do (pray about everything, with thanksgiving) and a promise (God's peace will guard your heart and mind). Many people also hold on to 1 Peter 5:7 and Isaiah 41:10.
Is it a sin to feel anxious?
Feeling anxious isn't a sin. The psalmists, Paul, and Jesus Himself in the garden of Gethsemane felt deep distress. Scripture's invitation is to bring that anxiety to God rather than carry it alone.
