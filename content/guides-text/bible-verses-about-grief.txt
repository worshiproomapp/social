Bible verses about grief and loss
Bible verses about grief and loss
By the Worship Room team · November 10, 2026
The Bible doesn't rush grief. Jesus wept at His friend's tomb (John 11:35). Psalm 34:18 promises that God is near to the brokenhearted, Matthew 5:4 says those who mourn will be comforted, and 1 Thessalonians 4:13 reminds believers that they grieve, but not "like the rest, who have no hope."

God is near in your grief
“Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit.”
Psalm 34:18
Closeness is the first promise, before answers or explanations.
“Jesus wept.”
John 11:35
The shortest verse in the Bible. Jesus knew He was about to raise Lazarus, and He wept anyway. Your tears are not a lack of faith.
“You count my wanderings. You put my tears into your container. Aren’t they in your book?”
Psalm 56:8
Not one tear is wasted or forgotten. God keeps track of every one.
“Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me. Your rod and your staff, they comfort me.”
Psalm 23:4
Through the valley, not around it. And not alone.
Comfort for the hurting
“Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted.”
Matthew 5:4
Jesus calls mourners blessed, not because grief is good, but because comfort is promised.
“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our affliction, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction, through the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.”
2 Corinthians 1:3–4
He is the "God of all comfort." Someday the comfort you receive may become comfort you can give.
“He heals the broken in heart, and binds up their wounds.”
Psalm 147:3
Healing for a broken heart takes time. God binds wounds like a careful physician.
“Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning.”
Psalm 30:5
The night can be long. This verse doesn't shorten it; it promises it will end.
Hope beyond loss
“I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will still live, even if he dies.”
John 11:25–26
Jesus said this to a grieving sister, before He wiped a single tear.
“But we don’t want you to be ignorant, brothers, concerning those who have fallen asleep, so that you don’t grieve like the rest, who have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep in Jesus.”
1 Thessalonians 4:13–14
Christians still grieve. But grief and hope can hold hands.
“Don’t let your heart be troubled. Believe in God. Believe also in me. In my Father’s house are many homes. If it weren’t so, I would have told you. I am going to prepare a place for you. If I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will receive you to myself; that where I am, you may be there also.”
John 14:1–3
A promise about home: a place prepared, and a reunion with Jesus.
“He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more; neither will there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain any more. The first things have passed away.”
Revelation 21:4
The last word over grief belongs to God: no more mourning, crying, or pain.
“For I am persuaded that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor things present, nor things to come, nor powers, nor height, nor depth, nor any other created thing will be able to separate us from God’s love which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”
Romans 8:38–39
Not even death can separate us from God's love.
A prayer for comfort in grief
God of all comfort, I miss them. Some moments I can't even find words. Be near to me like You promised. Hold the memories I'm afraid to lose, carry me through the hardest hours, and give me hope for the day when every tear is wiped away. Amen.
Gentle ways to grieve with God
Let yourself lament. Many psalms are prayers of sorrow. You can be honest with God about anger, confusion, and pain.
Write to God, or to the one you lost. A journal gives grief somewhere to go.
Don't grieve alone. Let a friend, your church, or a grief group walk with you. A counselor can help too, especially if grief feels stuck.
Expect waves. Grief doesn't follow a schedule. Holidays and anniversaries can bring it back. That's normal.
Frequently asked questions
What does the Bible say about grief?
The Bible treats grief as a real and holy part of love. Jesus wept (John 11:35), God is near to the brokenhearted (Psalm 34:18), and those who mourn are promised comfort (Matthew 5:4). Christians grieve with hope in the resurrection (1 Thessalonians 4:13–14).
What is a comforting Bible verse for the loss of a loved one?
Psalm 34:18, Revelation 21:4, and John 14:1–3 are among the most comforting. Many people also find Psalm 23 helpful at funerals and in the weeks after a loss.
Is it wrong for a Christian to grieve?
No. Grieving is not a lack of faith. Jesus Himself wept at the tomb of Lazarus, and Scripture is full of lament. Paul's encouragement is to grieve with hope, not to avoid grieving.
